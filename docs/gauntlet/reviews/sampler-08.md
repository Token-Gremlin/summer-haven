## Concrete findings

### 1. P2 — A journey observer can start during the new timed sampler without invalidating the result

`journey-observer.js:9` rejects only `__worldPerformance.running`, and its periodic check at `:156` likewise checks only that older sampler. The city sampler rejects an already-running observer at `city-activity-performance.js:15–16`, but its ongoing `check()` at `:154–165` never checks for one starting later. The observer's startup takes an exact world/storage snapshot (`journey-observer.js:19–31`); its regular sample reads full habitat snapshots, nearby resident state and DOM text (`:63–101`). Thus invoking it after the city sampler starts adds explicitly prohibited observation overhead while the city sampler can still report all six runs complete without a contamination reason.

Correct both directions: observer startup must reject either active sampler; city per-frame checks must reject a running observer or competing sampler, and the observer's ongoing guard should cover the new sampler. Preserve the record of an interrupted attempt. This is a source-proven guard gap, not a claim that contamination occurred. The externally held hardware lock remains necessary for other processes and recording tools.

### 2. P2 / J1–J2 support — Bicycle endpoint braking can turn into sustained reverse

`bike-route.js:36–39` holds S while waiting for `Math.abs(player.speed) < 0.1`, with observations every 100 ms (`:57`). Production cycling explicitly changes S from braking toward zero to reverse target `-0.7` once forward speed is at most `0.2` (`src/player/player.ts:41–46`). At normal frame cadence, speed can cross the helper's narrow ±0.1 stopping window between its 100 ms checks. Afterward the negative speed approaches -0.7 and never satisfies the stop condition. The finishing branch also precedes its progress/stall check, so reverse can continue until the overall 180-second cap.

Release S and end the helper when forward speed reaches a safe nonnegative threshold (including a negative-speed escape), allowing the normal controller to coast, or implement a bounded input-only brake controller. Do not write speed/position to stop it. Until repaired, an operator must monitor arrival, stop the helper promptly if it reverses and retain the actual input/movement record. This does not invalidate earlier route legs automatically, but a claimed stationary endpoint cannot be assumed from entering the finishing branch.

## Journey observer and bicycle helper boundaries

The observer performs reads, in-memory copies and event/listener bookkeeping. I found no player, camera, clock, world, UI, save-storage or renderer mutation in it. It records DOM `isTrusted`, bounded events/dropped counts and exact initial/final storage/world snapshots; its 45-minute timer and sample limits are explicit (`journey-observer.js:11–15,39–53,112–159`). Memory observations have no explicit array-length limit but are temporally bounded by the maximum session duration. It records camera samples, not every pointer movement, and deliberately cannot detect unlogged direct state writes (`:43`). Its support is S/R and coarse input/time evidence. It does not independently establish contiguous motion, normal-input authenticity, audio, or J1 completion. The concurrent sampler interlock requires finding 1's repair.

The bicycle helper uses synthetic W/S/A/D events (`bike-route.js:19–22,53–55`), and its only direct production mutation is disclosed camera yaw (`:56`). Production A/D steering and bicycle collision stay intact (`src/player/player.ts:41–54`); it does not teleport or write player speed, heading or world time. It bounds input waypoints to 40 and route time to 180 seconds, checks mode/hidden state, and releases keys it owns on a normal stop (`bike-route.js:8–35`). Its trace is bounded indirectly by that time and 100 ms period. It has no external-input arbitration or catch around each callback, so use one movement helper at a time with ordinary manual input released, keep the observer running, and stop/export on anomalies. Finding 2 is a real endpoint-control defect, not a movement bypass.
