## Largest remaining gap and next experiment

**For M:** unresolved connecting anatomy is the largest evidenced issue. The leg sleeve-like cutoffs in M-side/M-rest and cylindrical brow/faceted socket in M-face disrupt an otherwise coherent silhouette. Rebuild these transitions as intentional continuous forms; compare the same face and side views before evaluating decorative additions.

**For Q:** the remaining wing folding construction is the largest evidenced issue. In Q-rest, the near wing's long teal arm projects sideways/upward nearly straight, with thin gold spars collecting along the top of a dark triangular membrane bundle. The far wing similarly rises rather than reading as a relaxed folded limb. It reads more as a rearranged display wing than a compact anatomical resting wing. The still does not prove a simulation or rig defect, but the displayed result does not yet meet the close-craft target. The body-to-wing shoulder attachment also needs stronger anatomical transition. This affects D1 and Q4; any D2 judgment requires motion evidence.

Next experiment: author a clearly articulated shoulder–elbow–wrist–finger resting construction with believable membrane slack between spars, maintaining volume at the root. Show matched close root/wrist views, side and three-quarter folded silhouette and an extended-wing extreme. Keep Q's smoother eye/leg treatment. Then inspect a contiguous fold/unfold and flight/landing cycle in the actual runtime with player scale, material replacement and contact visible. Do not infer success from these stills or from new detail counts.

This report approves no complete mandatory gate and no whole-world claim.
