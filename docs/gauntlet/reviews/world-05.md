## Fixed gates

**Q1: FAIL for the shown region at the requested standard.** Approach has a clear bright-water focal point, dark foreground tree shadows and a midground sign/bench. Yet the huge cliff dominates almost the whole depth range; the thin visible receiving water and barely articulated banks do not give exploration the same clarity as the spectacle. Base has foreground boulders, player, falls and cliff, but the reachable route around the pool is unclear. Upstream has stronger atmospheric background depth, but the broad empty floor and obstructing central tree trunk do little to guide curiosity. The images are readable prototypes, not richly composed, connected exploration space.

**Q2: FAIL for the shown region.** L retains a more consistent simplified warm/painterly vocabulary, but its enormous flat cliff panels, sparse reeds, rounded moss lumps and water curtain remain conspicuously simple at player distance. N's added rock complexity does not consistently share the surface scale of the rest of the world. Particularly at base, noisy dark rock, diagonal stone grain, smooth water and lush green rounded moss compete rather than form one deliberate material family. Friendly player proportions and the warm/cool palette survive in both; that is a strength to retain.

This is an evidence limitation, not a claim that unseen topology is absent. Still mist glows support only presence of a visual effect. Flow layers, spray behavior, sound, collisions, navigation and the temple requirements remain unverified.

## Official-reference comparison

The independently inspected N2 image makes land and water explain one another: curving incised channels surround raised grass shelves, a bridge links two clearly readable paths, clusters of shrubs occupy banks and edges, and distant jagged mountains create a destination beyond the immediate crossing. Rock texture follows and reinforces the landforms while vegetation breaks long edges. This comparison is about those visible principles, not copying geometry or matching asset count. Its elevated wide camera, resolution, art direction and platform differ from this packet, so pixel-level equivalence is not a fair test.

Summer Haven currently communicates a giant waterfall behind the player more clearly than an explorable place shaped by water. The reference's most relevant advantage is the legibility and variety of the land-water boundary, not simply greater texture density.

## Largest remaining defect and next experiment

The largest shared defect is the weakly articulated **water-to-terrain relationship at human exploration scale**: a monumental smooth curtain against large cliff panels, with too little readable pool, eroded bank, downstream channel or inviting shelf route. This affects Q1/Q2 and the visible portion of W5. In N, surface noise makes this worse rather than compensating for it.

Run one focused geometry-and-composition experiment: author an asymmetric receiving basin with visible impact recess, two or three nested rock ledges, a downstream outlet and a walkable bank shelf that reveals their connection. Use restrained directional strata with a consistent material scale; eliminate the dark dotted rectangular face seen at N base. Place moss and reeds in shoreline clusters, leaving intentional open movement space. Keep the falls as the focal point and avoid adding unrelated props. Repeat the same three cameras with matched avatar heading and water phase, then add a contiguous normal walking segment from upstream bank to lip lookout and onward to pool/outlet. First judge silhouette, contact and route clarity; only then add finer stone texture. Motion, collision and localized audio require their own actual evidence.
