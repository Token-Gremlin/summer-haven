Remaining in J: most lower image area is broad gray road with large, flat, hard-edged shadow shapes; the plaster faces at upper-left center remain sparse rectangles; street-side green strips are clean bands; the central yellow building and right storefronts need more convincing ground-level use and material transitions. ## Falls pair

**K preferred, confidence high for rock shape.** J's upper-left and right cliff faces carry many elongated dark, grass-topped shelves at roughly repeated vertical intervals. Their thin horizontal silhouettes and isolated shadows read like attached platforms on a fairly smooth wall. K replaces that dominant impression with irregular upright masses: the left face has large angled planes, overlapping fractures, and a heavier rubble transition at bottom-left; the right side has a broken crest and changing widths. These volumes frame the central water more credibly and establish a more substantial landscape.

However, K's front-left plane (roughly left quarter, middle half) still has very large flat areas, the same diagonal surface grain continues over multiple faces, and dark narrow shard-like shapes below protrusions read awkwardly. The terrain looks like enlarged angular pieces rather than an integrated geological formation with a hierarchy of erosion, strata, cavities, vegetation and wet contact. The smooth rounded tree at upper-right sits in a markedly simpler shape language than the sharp nearby rock. The pale water remains essentially one broad, regularly streaked sheet in both images. Its upper lip is a straight sloping edge without an observable upstream channel; its bottom disappears at the frame edge with little readable collision, broken foam, or mist. A small right-base haze in K is insufficient to sell the volume of the fall. This critiques visible connection, not unseen upstream content.

The central fall is a clear focal point, and the cropped player, bench, and sign at the bottom provide scale. But the composition is primarily a frontal wall portrait: the bank route, receiving basin, downstream direction and traversable terraces are not legible. Unlike N2, this view does not let terrain, water, and route explain one another. W5 topology and navigation remain UNVERIFIED, not inferred absent from these crops.

## Largest remaining gap and next experiment

The largest visible gap is **the fall's land/water integration and surface hierarchy**, not the number of rock objects. It is still a patterned curtain against large faceted walls. This affects Q1, Q2 and Q4, with W5 requiring separate topology/motion evidence.

Next experiment: author one coherent waterfall cross-section from upstream lip through irregular wet rock to a visible plunge basin and bank route. Give it a few dominant rock masses, subordinate fracture/erosion detail, clustered vegetation on credible ledges, darker wet interfaces, and water breakup/foam where impact is visible. Preserve the painterly palette and player scale. Compare matched upstream, three-quarter approach, and base/route views, plus the same street control view, at declared unchanged settings; then provide contiguous traversal and water observation separately. The experiment succeeds only when the water's source, fall, impact and exit read as one place and rock/water/vegetation share an authored detail hierarchy. More facets or more decorative props alone do not satisfy it. The city still needs sustained skyline depth and materially authored street surfaces/use after its framing gain; it is not excused by choosing the falls as the next priority.
