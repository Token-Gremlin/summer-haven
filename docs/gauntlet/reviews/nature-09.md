## Pair findings

### Village river — prefer R, moderate confidence

Evidence: `K-village-river.png` and `R-village-river.png` in `evidence/nature-09/comparison-a/`.

Both preserve an excellent immediate route: the bridge leads from the broad foreground toward the village fork, with warm wood, cool water, green middle ground and softer mountains behind. R's river uses wider, quieter blue/teal shapes. K's numerous small white dashes and mottled highlights compete with the grass and canopy stipple, particularly below the left rail and across the right half. R better preserves the warm, restful scene hierarchy. R's large crown above the right-hand house has a less spherical, more irregular profile; the neighboring crown silhouettes also look less mechanically rounded.

The tradeoff is that R's broad smooth pale streaks can read as smeared bands, particularly at upper right; K offers more small-scale water cues. Neither proves current direction from a still. In both, the opposite bank is a straight, sharply bounded green shelf with repeated boulders. There is little visible shallow-water transition, bank shadow/contact or vegetation response to the edge. Groundcover remains a dispersed scatter of small, dark marks. No route obstruction is visible, but passability is untested.

Correction: keep R's restrained surface hierarchy, then make the existing bank/water contact readable through a narrow, irregular shallow/contact transition and restrained accents tied to actual banks or obstacles. Avoid putting fine foam uniformly across calm midstream water. Preserve the bridge and clear path widths.

### Mosswood — prefer M, moderate confidence

Evidence: `S-mosswood.png` and `M-mosswood.png`.

Ignoring deer locations and the toast, M has more short, splayed foreground tufts where S has conspicuous vertical reed-like slivers. This better suits a walkable woodland floor and reduces the impression of upright needles. M's canopy perimeter has more uneven lobes and exposes a little more branch structure near the top center. Warm trunks, dark green canopy and sunlit path remain compatible with the village palette. The right-hand path is immediately legible in both; canopy overlap supplies foreground framing and the opening at the center supplies depth.

The remaining problem is the large jump from very heavy, near-continuous canopy masses to thin, isolated floor tufts. Both show broad, almost empty flat ground broken mostly by patterned shadows. The tiny repeated leaf flecks do not substitute for clear intermediate branches and shrub/understory groupings. M is an incremental shape improvement, not a completed woodland craft pass. Existing shadows also carry much of the floor composition, which should be checked while the sun and camera move.

Correction: redistribute existing groundcover into several legible groups around selected roots/rocks and leave deliberate quiet gaps beside the path. Use existing geometry/population budget for a few broad low forms between grass and tree scale. Break selected crown masses with larger negative spaces and branch-supported clusters; do not multiply tiny lobes or add global density.

### Silverveil Falls — prefer D, high confidence for receiving-pool appearance

Evidence: `D-falls.png` and `P-falls.png`.

The falling sheet, cliff silhouette and framing are largely shared. The pale vertical fall remains the focal point against warm rock; the small player, bench and sign establish scale. Both retain an open foreground approach. P's rim trees are a little more irregular, but they are too small in this view to outweigh the pool issue.

At the base, D presents a blue-green receiving pool with restrained horizontal marks. P replaces much of that visible pool with a broad pale cream band, nearly the color of the falling sheet and light path. Its near-horizontal edges and abrupt notches around the left impact side make it read as a flat slab or surface overlay. It weakens the separation between falling water, impact and downstream water. This is an observable appearance issue; the still does not establish its implementation cause. D's mist is also visible at the lower right of the sheet, but the mist and impact are still too undifferentiated to convey convincing turbulence. Neither view proves upstream continuity or a coherent outlet.

Correction: preserve blue-green water beyond the impact zone. Confine bright aerated water to an irregular, localized impact patch that softens into the pool, with larger quiet blue intervals downstream. Check the lip, impact and outlet from above, base and along the bank, including movement; do not approve a cream strip merely because it is labeled foam.
