## Decision

**Accept the profiler as a useful bounded diagnostic contribution, with the limitations below. Accept the route records as narrow evidence that the two declared centreline walks completed. Do not approve P1–P4, J1–J3, L1, W3 or artistic quality from this packet.** The source-level bird migration is appropriately constrained to authored old contacts and trajectories; its runtime migration and visual contact remain unverified by this critic. The largest performance experiment is a controlled scene-rendering optimization, followed by matched production measurements and fidelity inspection. The largest remaining trail validation is real camera/contact observation across these routes and an old-save reload.

## Instrumentation and statistics

Inspected `src/main.ts`, `src/render/profiler.ts`, `tests/profiler.test.ts`, the post-render profiling boundaries, exact injected `evidence/profile-method-06.js`, `evidence/profile-06.json`, `tools/qa/draw-inventory.js` and `evidence/draw-city-06.json`.

The opt-in path constructs the profiler only for `?prof=1`. CPU stages are sequential; pass CPU timings are nested in those stages. GPU elapsed queries are asynchronous and separate from CPU time and RAF delivery. The renderer uses manual information resets, with scene/shadow/post counters spanning the appropriate render calls. The profiler bounds pending queries at 128, expires unavailable queries after 240 frames, discards outstanding disjoint results, prevents reuse following context loss and clears old-window queries at reset. Unsupported timing does not become a fabricated GPU zero. The supplied unit tests exercise these important boundaries, including the disabled path; I inspected them but did not run them.

All four raw runs name **index-BEBUlrp2.js**, Intel UHD 0x00009BC4 / ANGLE D3D11, Chrome 154, CSS viewport 920×668, DPR 1.25 and internal **805×584**. ### Validity gaps

- The fixed P2 protocol is not met. No matched profiler-off run measures instrumentation overhead, and no equivalent baseline/candidate pair establishes a speedup. The original 896×504 baseline differs in content and viewport. No performance claim transfers to newer source such as the currently edited sky partitioning or gait work.
- The helper's `laps` includes warmup traversal. Reset that counter or save its warmup value before using it as timed-window route completion. Retain waypoint events and the timed starting position. The raw one-second trace is useful but misses brief events.
- The helper resets world elapsed, city, habitat and siltfin state, but does not snapshot/restore all affected simulation state. The final settings/position restoration does not restore discoveries, world elapsed, animal/citizen states or necessarily their persisted copies. Use a named disposable QA save and record its identifier/seed, or fully restore a snapshot. Timing loops are not persistence journeys.
- Exact CPU model, physical RAM verification, driver version, refresh/vsync and a save/seed identifier are absent. Browser logical-processor/device-memory hints are not a complete hardware declaration. Actual population/visibility counts, natural schedule evolution and full dragon encounter coverage are not established by these local routes. Home is absent.
- Used-heap measurements are sampled, not a continuous peak or settled-loop measure. City ends at 404,592,407 bytes and forest at 408,592,667, above the 350 MB settled ceiling if these were settled values; later runs fall to roughly 345 MB. The largest retained one-second heap sample is 419,198,395 bytes, below 450 MB but not proof that no higher transient occurred. Geometry counts rise 438→626 as routes populate resources. No three-loop plateau, process/GPU memory, cold/warm loading budget or leak conclusion follows. P4 remains unverified with a concrete heap concern.

## Draw inventory

The inventory is useful for ranking submitted geometry: 456,962 scene triangles and 490,505 shadow triangles in the selected passes. Two unnamed `Group#20` meshes submit 67,360 and 67,120 scene triangles—about 29.4% of that scene inventory. They are a sensible object-identification/optimization lead, not proof of 29.4% GPU time. Do not infer their identity solely from unstable numeric object IDs or newer working source.

The capture chooses the first renderer frame separately by pass: scene **58707**, shadow **58706**. It is not a same-frame whole-frame total. The callback counts attempted submissions, including zero-instance records; renderer calls and callback records need not match one-for-one. It only wraps meshes under `g.scene`, so excludes post-processing scenes. Pass labeling assumes an orthographic camera means shadow and perspective means scene: valid for this specific reflection-off packet, inadequate for generalized reflection/other-camera inventories. The metadata lacks camera, time, viewport/internal size and exact scene state; record those plus stable mesh/geometry names and renderer-counter reconciliation on the next inventory. Submitted triangles alone say nothing about overdraw, shader cost, pixel coverage or visible artistic loss.

**Next performance experiment:** preserve BEBUlrp2's source/build identity, identify the two large geometry submissions with stable names, and compare a geometry-preserving culling/partition candidate under the same route, camera, state and settings. Run matched profiler-on diagnostics for pass attribution, then profiler-off three-by-60-second city/forest/waterfall/dragon measurements with raw samples and fixed warmups; include home for P1. ## Trail and bird-contact migration

Inspected `habitats.ts`, `habitat-save.ts`, `habitat-life.ts`, `habitat-migration.test.ts`, the current terrain-test fixture reference, `habitat-collision-06.json`, both trail route/recording metadata files, `tools/qa/walk-route.js`, and actual pixels of both `*-trail-start-06.png` images. The two WebM recordings are present but were **not played by this critic**. Their declared continuous motion and audio are not independently approved here. The collision fixture contains 6,016 obstacles and names BEBUlrp2; the terrain test now references fixture 06. Test passing is coordinator-reported, not independently executed here.

Three authored perch clusters move clear of the trails, while per-bird offsets are applied consistently to previous and current positions. Save validation extends only its broad bounds to admit the known old contacts; terrain-aware restore then matches old supports or the old interpolated flight within .03 m. Affected flights return to a real current perch, preserving cycle/cooldown while deliberately restarting phase. New valid flights are checked first and retain their phase. Arbitrary unsupported stationary positions are rejected. This is a defensible targeted compatibility policy, but it is not continuity of the old affected flight and must not be described that way.

The tests meaningfully check whole trail width plus a player margin, old perched/reacting history, old affected flights, new phase preservation including takeoff/landing, and rejection of an unrelated position. Missing focused cases include an old unaffected-node-to-unaffected-node flight, an incoming old flight from an unchanged node to a relocated endpoint, and malformed trajectories near the migration tolerance. The migration fixtures use `blocked: () => false`; current real-obstacle fixtures and gameplay reload should establish that relocated supports/routes are safe in the integrated world. No severe source defect was established in this narrow review.

The forest record reports the direct line from (196,-492) through (214,-508.286) to (228,-520.952), then back, with all 362 logged samples in play mode; the route finishes in 38.009 seconds within .334 m of its starting waypoint. The ridge record goes from (454,-996) to (488,-969.391) and back; all 364 logged samples are play mode, finishing in 36.710 seconds within .335 m. The walker drives ordinary W movement and collision with scripted camera yaw, without route coordinate writes. These state traces support **narrow centreline traversal success**, unlike the detour in profile-06. They do not prove a whole-region journey or quality of gait/camera transitions.

Both visual recordings declare BEBUlrp2, High, internal 1150×835, frozen time .12, diagnostic initial placement, profiling enabled and concurrent authoring. They are not performance evidence. The forest starting image shows the moved stones beside a clear straight trail, but a large Mosswood sign obstructs the foreground player/camera; determine whether normal approach causes persistent obstruction. The ridge starting image shows a readable uphill route. Neither single frame establishes animation or camera recovery. Reference N2's coherent path/terrain composition remains relevant, but these views alone cannot approve W3/Q1 craftsmanship.

**Next trail/contact experiment:** independently watch both full existing videos, then load actual old perched and in-flight saves on the current build, record the resulting support contact and departure, and walk both trail edges as well as centrelines in both directions with ordinary camera control. Include the Mosswood sign approach/recovery and a bicycle pass. Record before/after save state and reload without resetting habitats. This can close a local J2/W1 migration contribution; J1, full J2/J3 and L1 still require their complete behavioral evidence.
