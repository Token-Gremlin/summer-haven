## Blind decision

**Prefer K overall: strongly in river, modestly in deer.** This decision was sent to the coordinator before identity disclosure. The preference is useful, but is not reference-level or full-gate approval.

- Deer: K gives the neck, chest and body more form through restrained plane shading. M's broad orange torso/neck reads flatter and closer to a cutout. Both retain readable ear, eye, muzzle and contrasting underside; K's brown-gold is slightly more integrated with the forest. Both have very angular thin legs, abrupt shoulder/chest transitions and square spots that appear applied without following body curvature. The static pose has limited expression. Foreground hooves appear near ground, but a still cannot establish weight or foot contact through motion.
- River: K clearly communicates a turtle through visible head, eye, shell dome and limb silhouette. M shows essentially a small spotted disk at the same region of water, losing head/body identity. K therefore wins focal legibility decisively. K still appears placed against a nearly uniform water plane, with little visible waterline/contact disturbance. Water phase is unsynchronized, so this is not a claim of a new wake or simulation regression.

## Largest remaining gap and next experiment

The largest local craft gap is the deer anatomy/surface/pose: narrow angular limb sticks and abrupt chest/shoulder transitions remain prominent at close range. Author more deliberate joint transitions, hoof/leg volume and contour-following markings; retain K's readable form. Then capture front/three-quarter/side close and ordinary approach views plus uninterrupted rest-to-alert-to-walk/feeding sequences. For river, preserve readable turtle head and shell but test waterline placement, ripple/contact and swimming in uninterrupted ordinary play at near and approach distances. A separate broader river approach should show banks, route and depth before Q1 can be assessed beyond this crop.
