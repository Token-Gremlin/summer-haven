## Observable comparison

- In both images, cool blue water bends from upper center toward the lower right; the player and foreground tree provide readable scale. The broad water shapes, simple ground color masses and strong tree shadows retain a compatible stylized palette. The available dry foreground is legible, but this tight view cannot establish a complete bank trail or path-to-bridge connection.
- L64 adds a very conspicuous saturated green bank strip along the near diagonal edge: from approximately (565, 267), behind the player, to the lower-right corner. Its top has long straight segments and an abrupt corner near (1095, 514); a dark, nearly continuous lower edge makes it read as a raised curb or extruded landscape ribbon. It establishes more explicit relief, but that relief has a uniform constructed character rather than an irregular riverbank.
- L38 has a less explicit bank face. Its near edge blends into the olive ground with fewer competing color bands, so water, land and shadow feel more unified. However, the long diagonal contact still reads as a simple boundary between broad surfaces. L38 does not demonstrate convincing soil depth, shaped shallows or deliberately varied erosion either.
- L64's extra green strip competes with the quieter existing ground palette and exposes large planar facets. Its far bank and water remain similarly simplified to L38, so the added near-bank treatment is not yet a convincing coherent terrain language across the channel. The water pattern itself offers no clear preference in this matched still pair.

## Concrete N2 principle and remaining gap

The relevant documented N2 principle is that **land height, watercourse and traversable route should explain one another**: visible terraces make the channels' level understandable, while the path/bridge provides a readable way across. Copying its shapes or adding more regions is unnecessary. L64 makes the local height step easier to see, but a high-contrast, near-uniform green rim does not by itself achieve that relationship. These images do not show enough route context to verify the path component.

The largest remaining visible gap is **authored bank form and material contact**. The near bank needs controlled variation in slope, a few intentional changes in width/height, and a restrained transition from dry ground to the wet edge. In L64 especially, the long saturated strip and dark outline look applied to the landscape instead of formed by it. More scattered grass would not resolve this structural problem.

Next narrow experiment: reshape and soften this existing near-bank transition within its current footprint, reducing the uniform ribbon effect and coordinating its color with the far bank. Retain clear water/land separation and existing open travel space. Repeat this exact still view plus a low oblique bank view; then inspect camera movement and physical contact separately.
